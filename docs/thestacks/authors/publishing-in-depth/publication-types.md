# Publication types

The Stacks aims to expand the definition of what is "publishable", and enable rapid publishing of a variety of outputs from the scientific process.
Its goal is to enable flexible and continuous publishing, with embedded metadata giving the reader information about what was published, why, and what it means.

Each publication is tagged with a [Pub Type](#authors:pub-types).
Below is a brief summary of each, and examples for you to learn from.

(pub-type:idea)=
## Idea

A proposal or hypothesis you haven't tested yet.
Sharing it early invites collaborators and critique before you invest in the work.

:::{listing}
:source: stacks
:display: gallery
idea-prospective-biology
idea-defining-actin
:::

(pub-type:open-question)=
## Open question

A question you're stuck on, or one you think the field should answer.
It asks readers for their experience, data, or suggestions.

:::{listing}
:source: stacks
:display: gallery
open-question-measuring-reuse
open-question-ht-protein-expression-labor-yield
:::

(pub-type:observation)=
## Observation

Something unexpected you noticed but haven't fully explained.
It puts a curious result on the record so others can build on it.

:::{listing}
:source: stacks
:display: gallery
observation-chlamy-cpc1-growth
observation-marine-broth-chlamy
:::

(pub-type:method)=
## Method

A new or improved way to do something, described in enough detail to reuse.
It helps others adopt your approach without reinventing it.

:::{listing}
:source: stacks
:display: gallery
method-structural-mimicry
method-nematode-motility
:::

(pub-type:result)=
## Result

A finding from experiments or analysis, with the data behind it.
It's closest to a traditional paper, but can be smaller and faster to share.

:::{listing}
:source: stacks
:display: gallery
result-neural-network-yeast-global-epistasis
result-beads-cell-growth
:::

(pub-type:dataset)=
## Dataset

Data you collected or assembled, with enough context for others to reuse it.
It makes your data findable and citable on its own.

:::{listing}
:source: stacks
:display: gallery
dataset-raman-imaging-cerevisiae-mutants
dataset-archaea-giant-virus-protein-annotation
:::

(pub-type:resource)=
## Resource

A tool, software package, workflow, or other material that others can use directly.
It explains what the resource does and how to use it.

:::{listing}
:source: stacks
:display: gallery
resource-organism-illustration-library
resource-protein-cartography
:::

(pub-type:informative-failure)=
## Negative data and informative failures

An experiment or approach that didn't work, and what you learned from it.
It saves others from repeating the same dead end.

:::{listing}
:source: stacks
:display: gallery
negative-data-raman-batch-effects-yeast
informative-failure-fpguide
:::

(pub-type:perspective)=
## Perspective

Your view on a field, an approach, or how science gets done.
It starts conversations that don't fit in a research report.

:::{listing}
:source: stacks
:display: gallery
perspective-notebook-pub-format
perspective-icebox-lessons
:::
